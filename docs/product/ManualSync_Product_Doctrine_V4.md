# ManualSync Product Doctrine V3

## One product, full technical-documentation lifecycle
ManualSync must be a complete technical documentation platform: authoring, structured reuse, revision/change intelligence, product data, translation intelligence, publishing/layout, governance/release, integrations and adoption.

Do NOT frame the product as "not a translation tool". Translation is a first-class pillar and must be highly competitive. The constraint is operational: the ManualSync vendor must not be forced into running a translation agency. Customers can use their own agency, CAT/TMS, internal linguists or customer-configured AI/MT.

## Competitive rule
Do not attempt to win by having the longest feature list. Reach parity on table-stakes capabilities and win measurably on:
1. legacy-first onboarding and time-to-value;
2. revision/change intelligence and explainable reuse;
3. technical-safe exact/context/fuzzy matching;
4. zero-training guided UX;
5. layout-aware publishing and controlled release.

## Adoption rule
A customer must obtain useful results before a full migration. ManualSync must support coexistence with existing CCMS/TMS/Word/InDesign workflows and optional progressive replacement.

## UX rule
The normal user must not need to understand XML, DITA, XLIFF, TM internals or repository IDs.
Default = guided workflow + one recommended next action.
Advanced = scores, alignment, technical signatures, TM provenance, IDs and logs.

## Claims rule
Never say "better than Paligo/Tridion" without a benchmark. Never market alignment coverage as accuracy. Never call a capability complete because it compiles. Every claim must map to an acceptance test.


## Objection rule
Commercial objections are product requirements, not sales-script problems.
The product must explicitly neutralize:
- "we already have Paligo / another system" through coexistence, interoperability and measurable incremental value;
- "we already have suppliers" through provider neutrality and internal ownership of structure/memory/audit;
- "we are small / have few manuals" through proportional deployment and qualification (sometimes the correct answer is no sale);
- "we do everything internally" by removing repetitive non-engineering work from technical staff;
- "we have no time to learn" through zero-training guided UX benchmarks;
- "we use InDesign / Word / only PDF" through format-aware onboarding and publishing paths;
- "we do not want migration" through value-before-migration and progressive adoption.

Every objection must map to:
1. a product capability;
2. an acceptance test;
3. a measurable business outcome;
4. a clear "not available yet" label if still roadmap.
